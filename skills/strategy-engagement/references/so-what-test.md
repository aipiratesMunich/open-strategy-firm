# So-What Test

## What it is

A repeated question applied to a finding: "So what for this decision?" Each answer becomes the input for the next round, until the answer names an implication or an action for the client. It separates findings that matter from facts that are merely true. It answers: "Why does this finding deserve the reader's attention?"

## When to use

- Turning findings into conclusions during [Synthesis](synthesis.md).
- Checking every slide title and executive-summary line before release ([Action Titles](action-titles.md)).
- Deciding what goes into the main document and what goes to the appendix.
- Reviewing desk research and interview summaries before they enter the [Storyline and Ghost Deck](storyline.md).

## When NOT to use

- **Factual reference requests.** A client who asked for a market data book wants the data. Adding a "so what" to every table imposes an interpretation they did not request. Label the facts and stop.
- **Before the facts are checked.** Running the so-what chain on an unverified number builds a confident implication on sand. Verify first ([Triangulation](triangulation.md), [Weak Evidence](weak-evidence.md)).
- **When the implication belongs to someone else.** In a fact-finding mandate (due diligence input, audit support), the client draws the conclusions. State findings with their significance level, not recommendations.
- **To force a recommendation out of every finding.** Some findings support a conclusion without needing their own action. Group them instead ([Pyramid Principle](pyramid-principle.md)).

## Step by step

1. **State the finding** as a full sentence with number, unit, period and source. Input: analysis output. Output: one finding line.
2. **Ask "so what?" once.** Output: the direct consequence for the client's business (revenue, cost, risk, position).
3. **Ask again.** Output: the consequence for the decision in the problem statement.
4. **Stop when the answer names an action or a change in the answer.** Usually two to three rounds. If round four still produces facts, the finding is not relevant to this decision.
5. **Quantify the implication** where possible, with a range.
6. **Sort.** Findings with an implication go to the storyline. Findings without one go to the appendix, or are cut.
7. **Rewrite the slide title or summary line** as the implication, with the finding as support.

## Inputs needed

- Problem statement and decision.
- Verified findings with sources.

## Output produced

- For each finding: the so-what chain, the final implication, a quantified effect where possible, and a sort decision (main line, appendix, cut).

## Common mistakes

- **Stopping at round one.** "Prices are 6% below competitors, so Alder is cheap." That restates the finding. A partner asks the next "so what".
- **Implication that is a generic truth.** "So customers value reliability." Every industrial buyer does. The implication must be specific to this client and decision.
- **Jumping to an action the finding cannot carry.** One interview quote leading to "restructure sales". The chain must hold at every step.
- **Keeping everything.** Appendix exists for a reason. Thirty interesting facts in the main line hide the five that decide.
- **Titles that describe the chart.** "Revenue by segment 2022-2025" fails the test by design.

## Worked example

*Fictional example. Company, figures and people are invented.*

Alder Pumps, two findings:

**Finding A:** "Alder's list prices are 6% below its two main competitors for comparable pumps (price benchmark, 2025)."
- So what? Alder earns less per pump than the market would bear.
- So what? There is room for a price increase without becoming the expensive option.
- So what? A 4% increase in 2027 keeps Alder about 2% below competitors and adds about EUR 7m revenue if volume holds.
- Action: recommend the 4% increase; monitor order intake for two quarters. Main line.

**Finding B:** "Customer satisfaction score is 7.8 of 10, stable since 2022 (client survey)."
- So what? Customers are moderately satisfied.
- So what? No change, no outlier against the price or service questions.
- No implication for growth or pricing. Appendix.

## Output template

```markdown
# So-what chains: <decision>

| # | Finding (number, unit, period, source) | So what? (1) | So what? (2) | So what? (3) | Implication / action | Effect (range) | Sort |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | | | | | | | main / appendix / cut |

**Rewritten titles:**
- Finding <n>: "<implication as full-sentence title>"
```
