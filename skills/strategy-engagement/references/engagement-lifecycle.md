# Engagement Lifecycle

## Purpose

The end-to-end sequence from a raw briefing to a decision the client acts on. It exists so that no phase is skipped and every phase hands a defined artefact to the next. Without it, teams start analysing before they know the question and finish with facts nobody asked for.

## Entry criteria

- A briefing exists: a written or spoken request from someone who owns a decision.
- The person who will act on the answer is known by name or role.

## Step by step

1. **Frame** ([Problem Definition](problem-definition.md)). Turn the briefing into one problem statement with decision maker, success criteria, scope and deadline. Output: signed-off problem statement.
2. **Structure** ([Hypothesis-Driven Approach](hypothesis-driven-approach.md)). Break the question into an issue tree, write a first answer for each branch, and draft a ghost deck ([Storyline and Ghost Deck](storyline.md)) that shows what the final story would look like if the hypotheses hold. Output: issue tree, hypotheses, ghost deck.
3. **Plan** ([Workplan](workplan.md)). Prioritise branches by how much they can move the decision, assign analyses, sources and deadlines. Output: workplan.
4. **Gather and analyse**. Run analyses and interviews ([Interview Guide](interview-guide.md)) branch by branch. Each analysis either confirms, kills or reshapes a hypothesis. Update the ghost deck after every material finding. Output: analysis results with sources.
5. **Synthesise** ([Synthesis](synthesis.md)). Turn findings into a small number of supported conclusions and one recommendation. Output: storyline with action titles and executive summary.
6. **Review** ([Partner Review Checklist](partner-review-checklist.md)). Partner review at three gates: after Frame, after Structure, and before anything leaves the team. Output: "release" or "return with comments".
7. **Decide** ([Steering Committee and Final Presentation](steerco-and-final-presentation.md)). Pre-wire stakeholders, then present to the decision body and record the decision. Output: decision log and next steps.
8. **Hand over**. Document owners, milestones and the first 90 days of implementation. Output: implementation plan.

Loop rule: steps 2 to 5 run as fast iterations, not a waterfall. A first end-to-end answer should exist on day one, however rough, and improve each cycle.

## Inputs needed

- The briefing and any documents the client provides.
- Access to at least one person who can answer questions about the business.
- Data the client can share, or permission to work with public data and flagged assumptions.

## Output produced

- Problem statement, issue tree, hypotheses, ghost deck, workplan.
- Analyses with a source for every number.
- Final deck or memo, model, decision log, implementation plan.

## Exit criteria

- The decision maker has made, or explicitly deferred, the decision named in the problem statement.
- Every claim in the final document traces to an analysis or a named source.
- The final document passed partner review with no open blocker.
- Owners and dates exist for every next step.

## Common mistakes

- **Analysis before framing.** Collecting data before the question is agreed. Most wasted work in projects traces back here.
- **Waterfall thinking.** Finishing all research before writing any answer. The answer arrives late and untested.
- **Boiling the ocean.** Treating every branch of the issue tree as equally important.
- **Review at the end only.** A partner who sees the work for the first time two days before the steering committee can only polish, not redirect.
- **No decision named.** A project that ends with "insights" rather than a decision has no way to judge success.

## Worked example

*Fictional example. Company, figures and people are invented.*

Alder Pumps, a family-owned maker of industrial pumps with EUR 180m revenue, sends a briefing: "Revenue has been flat for three years. We want to grow." The engagement manager frames it as: "Which two growth moves should the board fund in the 2027 budget to reach EUR 200m revenue by 2030 with EBIT margin not below 9%?" On day one the team writes a rough answer (expand service contracts for the installed base, enter the Polish market), builds an issue tree around it, and drafts a ten-page ghost deck. The partner reviews the frame and strikes the Polish branch as untested. Two weeks of analysis show service contracts can add roughly EUR 12.5m at a 40% take-up, a pricing reset about EUR 7m, and the Polish entry less than EUR 5m by 2030. The final storyline recommends service contracts plus a pricing reset, the board approves, and a 90-day plan names the head of service as owner.
