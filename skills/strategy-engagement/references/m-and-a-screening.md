# M&A Screening

## Typical questions

M&A questions often start from a target someone already likes, or a banker's list. The job is to move the client from "is this a good company?" to "does owning it, at this price, beat our alternatives?"

- "Find us acquisition targets in <space>." → "Which companies in <space> would advance <strategic goal> and could be bought and integrated within <budget> and <risk appetite>?"
- "Should we buy <target>?" → "Is <target> worth more to us than the price, counting only synergies we can name, own and deliver, and does it beat organic options?"
- "The banker says this target is a unique opportunity." → Same question, plus: what does the seller know that we do not?
- "We need to build scale quickly." → First test whether acquisition is the right route at all against organic growth and partnerships ([Growth Strategy](growth-strategy.md)).

An M&A question is sharp when it states the strategic goal the deal serves, the maximum price logic, and the deal-breakers agreed before any target is discussed.

## Issue tree skeleton

A MECE starting tree. Adapt, never use unchanged.

- **Why buy at all?**
  - The strategic goal: capability, market access, scale, technology, talent
  - Alternatives: build, partner, license. *H: building the capability in-house takes longer than the market window allows.*
- **Which targets fit?** (screening)
  - Must-have criteria: segment, size range, geography, ownership, availability
  - Deal-breakers: legal, regulatory, customer concentration, key-person dependence, culture or governance conflicts
- **Strategic fit**
  - Does the target close the stated gap? Overlap and complementarity in products, customers, channels, capabilities ([Value Chain](value-chain.md))
  - Can we integrate it, or must it run standalone?
- **Financial fit**
  - Standalone value without synergies. *H: standalone value is below the asking price.*
  - Synergies: cost (overlap you can remove) versus revenue (sales you hope to add), each with owner, timing and integration cost
  - Affordability: price, financing, effect on leverage and earnings
- **Execution risk**
  - Integration complexity, key people, systems, customer reaction, competitor reaction
- **Walk-away logic**
  - Maximum price = standalone value plus realistic net synergies; above that, walk

Keep strategic fit and financial fit as separate branches. A strong strategic story is no reason to pay above the walk-away price.

## Key analyses

- **Screening funnel.** Long list to short list with explicit, weighted criteria and documented deal-breakers ([Competitor Profiling](competitor-profiling.md) for each target profile).
- **Market and position of target.** [Market Sizing](market-sizing.md) and [Five Forces](five-forces.md) for the target's segment; is its growth market growth or share gain?
- **Strategic fit map.** Where the target's [Value Chain](value-chain.md) adds to or overlaps with the buyer's; [Ansoff Matrix](ansoff-matrix.md) to name the type of move.
- **Standalone valuation.** [Business Case](business-case.md) of the target on its own plan, then on a haircut plan built from its history.
- **Synergy case.** Cost and revenue synergies separately, each with driver, owner, timing, one-off cost; [Scenario Planning](scenario-planning.md) with a no-revenue-synergy case.
- **Deal-breaker check.** Customer concentration, change-of-control clauses, regulatory approvals, pending litigation, key-person risk.
- **Outside view.** How comparable deals by this buyer or in this sector performed against their own cases ([Weak Evidence](weak-evidence.md)).

## Traps

- **Revenue synergies treated as certain.** Practitioners widely warn that revenue synergies are rarely realised as planned: customers do not buy more because two companies merged, sales teams resist cross-selling, and competitors react. Value them as upside, outside the walk-away price.
- **Cost synergies without owners or integration cost.** Every synergy needs a named owner, a date and its one-off cost. Without them it is a hope.
- **Deal momentum.** Once a team, bankers and a board are invested, walking away feels like failure. Agree walk-away price and deal-breakers before due diligence starts, and have someone outside the deal team review the case.
- **Anchoring on the asking price.** Valuing the target to justify the seller's number instead of building value from the buyer's own view.
- **Screening on availability.** Ranking targets by which ones are for sale shrinks the list to what bankers bring. Screen the full field first.
- **Strategic fit stands in for diligence.** A perfect fit does not fix weak unit economics, a fragile customer base or a culture that will lose key people.
- **Ignoring the integration plan.** Who runs it, what changes on day one, what stays untouched. A deal without an integration thesis is unpriced risk.

## Worked example

*Fictional example. Company, figures and people are invented.*

Halvorsen Instruments, a Nordic lab-equipment maker with about EUR 400m revenue, wants software capabilities for connected lab devices. Sharpened question: "Which software company would give us a connected-lab offer within two years, at a price below standalone value plus net cost synergies?" Deal-breakers agreed upfront: no target with more than 30% revenue from one customer; no unresolved IP ownership.

Funnel: 60 companies on the long list, 15 after must-have criteria, 5 after strategic fit, 2 approached. The preferred target asks EUR 120m. Standalone value on a haircut plan: about EUR 100m. Cost synergies of about EUR 3m per year (shared hosting, overlapping sales offices) are worth about EUR 15m after EUR 5m integration cost. Walk-away price: about EUR 115m. The seller's case adds EUR 6m per year of revenue synergies from selling software to Halvorsen's installed base; the team treats these as upside and keeps them out of the price. Diligence then finds 40% of target revenue with one pharma customer whose contract has a change-of-control clause, which breaches the agreed deal-breaker. Recommendation: do not proceed at any price unless that customer signs a contract extension before signing; in parallel, assess the second target.

## Playbook

How to run this case step by step. Steps 1 to 4 are specific to this case type; the shared engagement procedure covers the rest.

### Step 1: Sharpen the question

M&A questions often start from a target someone already likes. Move the client to "does owning it, at this price, beat our alternatives?" Ask, in small groups:
- Which strategic goal must the deal serve: capability, market access, scale, technology, talent? By when?
- Why not build, partner or license? How long would building take?
- What is the budget, and what effect on debt and earnings is acceptable?
- Which deal-breakers apply regardless of price (customer concentration, unresolved IP, regulatory approval, key-person dependence, governance)?
- Who on the team, the board or the bank is already committed to a target? Who outside the deal team will review the case?
- For a banker's target: what might the seller know that you do not?

Write the decision question in this form: "Is <target> (or: which company in <space>) worth more to us than the price, counting only synergies we can name, own and deliver, and does it beat organic options?" It is sharp when it states the strategic goal, the maximum price logic and the deal-breakers. Agree deal-breakers and the walk-away logic now, before any target is discussed.

### Step 2: Instantiate the issue tree

Keep strategic fit and financial fit as separate branches. Adapt the skeleton in the case note; set each threshold with the client.

| Branch | Hypothesis (example form) | Kill criterion |
|---|---|---|
| Why buy | Building the capability in-house takes longer than the market window | A build, partner or license route reaches the goal in time for less |
| Screen | At least <n> companies meet the must-have criteria | Full field screened and none passes: revisit goal or route |
| Deal-breakers | The preferred target passes every agreed deal-breaker | Any deal-breaker breached and not cured before signing |
| Strategic fit | The target closes the stated gap; overlap and complementarity are clear | Target adds little the buyer lacks, or cannot be integrated or run standalone |
| Standalone value | Standalone value on a haircut plan is close to the asking price | Standalone value well below asking price with no cost synergies to bridge it |
| Cost synergies | Named cost synergies, net of integration cost, bridge the gap | Synergies lack owners, dates or one-off costs |
| Execution | Integration risk is manageable with named owners | Key people, systems or customers likely to leave on change of control |
| Walk-away | Price stays at or below standalone value plus net cost synergies | Price above walk-away: walk |

### Step 3: Run the deciding analyses, in order

Each analysis ends by marking its hypothesis confirmed, killed or reshaped. If your agent can run sub-tasks in parallel, profile short-list targets in parallel; otherwise in order.

a. **Screening funnel.** Full field first, then must-haves, weights and deal-breakers, documented per stage; a profile per target ([Competitor Profiling](competitor-profiling.md)).
b. **Target market and position.** [Market Sizing](market-sizing.md) and [Five Forces](five-forces.md) for the target's segment: is its growth market growth or share gain?
c. **Strategic fit map.** Where the target's [Value Chain](value-chain.md) adds to or overlaps with the buyer's; name the move type with the Ansoff grid; for a multi-business buyer, its place in the portfolio ([Growth-Share Matrix](growth-share-matrix.md)).
d. **Standalone valuation.** [Business Case](business-case.md) on the target's plan, then on a haircut plan built from its history.
e. **Synergy case.** Cost and revenue synergies separately, each with driver, owner, timing and one-off cost; [Scenario Planning](scenario-planning.md) including a no-revenue-synergy case; [Sensitivity Analysis](sensitivity-analysis.md) on the largest synergy.
f. **Deal-breaker check.** Customer concentration, change-of-control clauses, approvals, litigation, key-person risk.
g. **Outside view.** How comparable deals by this buyer or in this sector performed against their own cases. Without data, name the missing comparison.

### Step 4: Run the trap checks

Answer each with yes or no and fix every no before writing:
- [ ] Revenue synergies are shown as upside only and are outside the walk-away price.
- [ ] Every cost synergy has an owner, a date and its one-off integration cost.
- [ ] Walk-away price and deal-breakers were agreed before diligence and are unchanged; any change is logged with its reason.
- [ ] Value is built from the buyer's own view, not back-solved from the asking price.
- [ ] The full field was screened, not only targets that are for sale.
- [ ] Strategic fit is not used to excuse weak unit economics, fragile customers or key-person risk ([Logic Gaps](logic-gaps.md)).
- [ ] An integration thesis exists: who runs it, what changes on day one, what stays untouched.
- [ ] Seller and banker numbers are graded as such ([Weak Evidence](weak-evidence.md)); the valuation passes [Model Red Flags](model-red-flags.md).

### Step 5: Answer page

Use the output template in [M&A Screening](m-and-a-screening.md) for the body, wrapped in the shared format below. The answer is proceed, do not proceed, or proceed under named conditions, with the maximum price.

### Case rules

- "Do not proceed at any price unless <condition>" is a full answer. When only seller or banker figures exist (teaser, IM without data room), give no valuation, cap or maximum price; the answer is the next step and the conditions under which a price can be set.

### Limits

This is a screening and decision case, not legal, tax, financial or technical due diligence; flag where each is needed. Valuation here supports a walk-away price, not a fairness opinion.
