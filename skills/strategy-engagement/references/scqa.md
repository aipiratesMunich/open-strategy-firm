# SCQA

## What it is

A four-part opening for a document or presentation: Situation, Complication, Question, Answer. It brings the reader from what they already accept to the question the document answers, then gives the answer. It answers: "Why should this reader care about this document now?"

## When to use

- The first paragraph of an executive summary, memo or deck introduction.
- When the audience does not yet share the team's view of the problem and must agree on it before hearing the answer.
- Framing a problem statement at the start of a project, to check the team and client agree on the complication ([Problem Definition](problem-definition.md)).

## When NOT to use

- **Audience already asked the question.** If the CEO requested "your recommendation on pricing", restating situation and complication wastes the first page. Lead with the answer ([Pyramid Principle](pyramid-principle.md)).
- **Status updates and progress reports.** There is no complication to build; use a short status format (done, next, decisions needed).
- **As the structure of the whole deck.** SCQA opens a document. The body follows the key line of the pyramid; stretching SCQA over 30 slides buries the answer on slide 25.
- **When the complication is invented.** If nothing has changed and no tension exists, a manufactured complication reads as a sales pitch. Ask whether the document is needed at all.

## Step by step

1. **Situation.** Input: briefing, facts the reader already knows. Output: one or two sentences the reader will nod at. No new information, no opinion.
2. **Complication.** Output: one or two sentences on what changed or what tension exists that makes the situation unstable. This is where the reader starts to care.
3. **Question.** Output: the single question the complication raises. It should match the problem statement. Often it stays implicit in the final text.
4. **Answer.** Output: the governing thought of the [Pyramid Principle](pyramid-principle.md), in one sentence.
5. **Test the chain.** Does the complication follow from the situation? Does the question follow from the complication? Does the answer answer that exact question? If any link fails, rewrite before writing the body.
6. **Choose the order for the audience.** Standard order (S, C, Q, A) for an audience that needs to be led. Answer-first order (A, then S and C as context) for an impatient decision maker.

## Inputs needed

- Problem statement and decision maker.
- The governing thought from the synthesis.
- What the audience already knows and believes.

## Output produced

- An opening paragraph of four to six sentences.
- A checked match between the question and the answer.

## Common mistakes

- **Situation that contains news.** If the reader learns something new in the situation, it belongs in the complication. A partner strikes it because it blurs where the tension starts.
- **Complication that is a symptom list.** "Revenue flat, margins under pressure, competitors active" is three facts, not one tension. Pick the one that drives the question.
- **Question that does not match the answer.** The complication raises "where to grow?" and the answer is about cost. A reviewer catches this in the first minute.
- **Answer that is a plan to find the answer.** "We will analyse the options" is not an answer.
- **Too long.** An SCQA that runs to a page has stopped being an opening.

## Worked example

*Fictional example. Company, figures and people are invented.*

Alder Pumps, board memo opening:

- **Situation:** Alder has held revenue at EUR 180m for three years with an EBIT margin of about 9.5%, and the board has set a target of EUR 200m by 2030 with no acquisitions and at most EUR 20m capital.
- **Complication:** The core pump business is not growing, and the obvious expansion route, Poland, is closed: two local players hold 78% and entry would add less than EUR 5m by 2030.
- **Question (implicit):** Which organic levers can close a EUR 20m gap within the capital limit?
- **Answer:** A service-contract offer on the installed base and a 4% list-price increase in 2027 add about EUR 19.5m, which reaches the target in the base case with almost no cushion; the board decides in March 2027 whether to fund a further lever as a buffer.

Chain test: the complication follows from the situation (flat core, target requires growth), the question follows from the complication, the answer names levers and a number against the gap.

## Output template

```markdown
# Opening: <document title>

**Situation:** <what the reader already knows and accepts>

**Complication:** <what changed or what tension makes the situation unstable>

**Question:** <the one question this raises; matches the problem statement>

**Answer:** <governing thought, one sentence>

**Order used:** S-C-Q-A / A-S-C

**Chain check:**
- [ ] Situation contains no new information
- [ ] Complication is one tension, not a list
- [ ] Question follows from the complication
- [ ] Answer answers exactly that question
```
