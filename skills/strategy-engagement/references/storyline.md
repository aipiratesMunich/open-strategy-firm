# Storyline and Ghost Deck

## Purpose

Write the final document's story before the analysis is done, as a sequence of slide titles with empty or sketched bodies. The ghost deck shows which analyses the story needs and which ones it does not, so the team works backwards from the answer. It is rewritten every time a hypothesis dies, and at the end becomes the real deck.

## Entry criteria

- Problem statement and day-one answer exist.
- First hypotheses are written ([Hypothesis-Driven Approach](hypothesis-driven-approach.md)).

## Step by step

1. **Write the opening** using [SCQA](scqa.md): situation everyone agrees with, complication that creates tension, the question that follows, and the answer. Four sentences.
2. **Write the governing thought:** the answer in one sentence. This is the title of the executive summary.
3. **List the key line:** three to five statements that together prove the governing thought. Each becomes a section.
4. **Write one action title per page** under each section ([Action Titles](action-titles.md)). Titles are full sentences stating the finding, written as if the hypothesis were confirmed.
5. **Sketch the body** of each page in one line: the chart or table that would prove the title, and its data source. "Bar chart: installed base by age band, ERP export."
6. **Read the titles alone, in order.** They must tell the whole story without the bodies (horizontal logic). If they do not, reorder or rewrite.
7. **Map pages to the workplan.** Every page needs an analysis; every analysis needs a page ([Workplan](workplan.md)).
8. **Mark the status of each page:** hypothesis, partly supported, proven, or killed. Killed pages trigger a rewrite of the governing thought.
9. **Re-run steps 2 to 6** after each material finding. Keep old versions so the change in the answer is traceable.

## Inputs needed

- Problem statement, day-one answer, hypothesis list.
- Later: findings and the synthesis pyramid ([Synthesis](synthesis.md)).

## Output produced

- Ghost deck: ordered list of action titles with body sketch, data source and status.
- Final storyline once findings are in.

## Exit criteria

- Titles alone tell the full story in a logical order.
- The opening follows SCQA and ends in the governing thought.
- Every page maps to an analysis and shows its status.
- No page title is a topic label ("Market overview").
- For the final version: every page is "proven" or explicitly flagged.

## Common mistakes

- **Topic titles.** "Competitive landscape" says nothing. "Two local players hold 78% of the Polish market, leaving no room for a mid-price entrant" says everything.
- **Ghost deck as decoration.** Written once in week one, never updated, while the real story drifts elsewhere.
- **Building backwards from available charts.** Pages exist because analyses were done, not because the story needs them.
- **Too long.** A 60-page story has no key line. Main deck stays short; detail goes to the appendix.
- **Confusing hypothesis with proof.** Presenting ghost titles to the client as findings before they are tested.

## Worked example

*Fictional example. Company, figures and people are invented.*

Alder Pumps ghost deck, day two. Opening: "Alder Pumps holds a strong position in Central European industrial pumps (S). Revenue has been flat for three years while the board targets EUR 200m by 2030 (C). Which growth moves should the board fund in 2027 (Q)? Service contracts on the installed base and a market entry in Poland (A)." Key line: (1) The installed base is a large untapped service market. (2) Customers will pay for guaranteed uptime. (3) Poland offers a second growth engine. Page 4 title: "More than 40% of the 60,000 installed pumps are older than eight years and have no service contract", body: bar chart by age band, ERP export, status: hypothesis. After week two, page 7 on Poland is killed by market-share data; key line 3 becomes "List prices can rise 4% without losing volume" and the governing thought is rewritten.
