# Jobs to Be Done

## What it is

A lens that explains purchase decisions by the progress a customer is trying to make in a specific situation, instead of by customer attributes. The job has functional, emotional and social sides, and the competition is whatever customers use today to get it done, including doing nothing. It informs what to offer, how to segment and what customers will pay for. It cannot tell you how many customers have the job or what they will pay; that needs [Market Sizing](market-sizing.md) and [Pricing Analysis](pricing-analysis.md).

## When to use

- Designing or repositioning an offer, especially a new service.
- Demographic or firmographic segments do not predict who buys.
- Explaining switching and churn: why customers leave or never start.
- Finding substitutes that a category view misses.

## When NOT to use

- **Commodity markets with spec-driven purchasing.** When buyers compare price per specification, a price and cost analysis tells more.
- **The question is volume or price level.** Use [Market Sizing](market-sizing.md) or [Pricing Analysis](pricing-analysis.md); jobs can shape the hypotheses but not the numbers.
- **No access to customers.** A job written from desk research is a guess. Run interviews first ([Interview Guide](interview-guide.md)).
- **Late-stage optimisation.** Fine-tuning a known offer is better served by tests and usage data.

## Step by step

1. **Pick the moment of choice.** Input: a recent purchase, switch or cancellation. Output: list of interviewees who made that choice recently, including those who chose an alternative.
2. **Reconstruct the situation.** Interview about what happened before the choice: trigger, what they tried, what they gave up, who else was involved. Output: one timeline per interview.
3. **Write job statements.** Output: "When <situation>, I want to <progress>, so that <outcome>", with functional, emotional and social sides noted.
4. **Map current solutions.** Output: what each interviewee uses today for the job, including workarounds and doing nothing.
5. **Group by job.** Output: segments defined by job and situation, with rough share of interviews.
6. **Translate into offer and price hypotheses.** Output: what the offer must do to be hired, and what it replaces, as testable hypotheses for [Pricing Analysis](pricing-analysis.md) and [Market Sizing](market-sizing.md).

## Inputs needed

- Access to 10 to 20 recent buyers, switchers and non-buyers.
- Sales and churn data to select interviewees.
- An interview guide focused on past behaviour.

## Output produced

- Job statements with situation, progress sought and current solutions.
- Job-based segments and offer hypotheses ready for sizing and pricing.

## Common mistakes

- **Jobs that are product features.** "Wants a pump with longer service intervals" describes a solution. Describe the progress.
- **Vague jobs.** "Wants reliability" cannot guide any trade-off. Add situation and outcome.
- **Asking what customers want.** Stated wishes are weak; reconstruct what they did ([Weak Evidence](weak-evidence.md)).
- **Only customers interviewed.** Non-buyers and switchers reveal the competing solutions.
- **No numbers afterwards.** A job without sizing does not support an investment.

## Worked example

*Fictional example. Company, figures and people are invented.*

Alder Pumps interviews plant managers about their last unplanned pump failure. Job statement: "When a critical pump fails mid-shift, I want the line running again within two days, so that I do not have to explain lost output to my plant director." Current solutions: own maintenance team (large plants), calling the nearest repair shop (mid-size plants), keeping a spare pump on the shelf. The emotional side is visible in the answers: the cost of a failure matters less than being blamed for it.

Implications: the offer should sell a response guarantee, which supports the 48-hour response feature. A maintenance schedule on its own misses the job. The competing solution for mid-size plants is the spare pump on the shelf, which gives a price anchor for the willingness to pay of about EUR 1,000 per pump per year. Large plants with their own teams are a different job and a smaller target.

## Output template

```markdown
# Jobs to be done: <offer or category>

**Moment of choice studied:** <purchase / switch / cancel>; <n> interviews (<buyers>, <switchers>, <non-buyers>)

| Job statement (When ..., I want to ..., so that ...) | Functional | Emotional / social | Current solutions | Share of interviews |
|---|---|---|---|---|

**Offer hypotheses:** <what the offer must do to be hired; what it replaces>
**To size and price next:** <hypothesis> → <analysis>
```
