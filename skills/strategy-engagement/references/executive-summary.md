# Executive Summary

## What it is

One page that gives the complete answer: the recommendation, the reasons, the size of the prize, the risks and the decision requested. Many readers will see nothing else. It must work without the presenter and without the rest of the document.

## When NOT to use

- **There is no answer yet.** An interim update has a status summary (what we know, what we do not know yet, next steps). A summary that pretends to conclude damages trust when the answer changes.
- **The whole document is one page.** A [One-Page Memo](one-page-memo.md) is its own summary; do not add a second.
- **Data books and appendices.** Give them an index, not a summary.

## Standard

- **Answer first.** The first sentence is the governing thought: what the client should do. No background before it.
- **SCQA framing in a few lines.** Situation, complication and question set up the answer briefly ([SCQA](scqa.md)); they may sit above the answer as a short lead-in, but the answer is visible without scrolling or turning.
- **Three to five supporting points**, one per key-line section, each a full sentence with its key number ([Pyramid Principle](pyramid-principle.md)).
- **Magnitude and comparison.** Value in euros or percent, compared with the target, the gap or the cost.
- **Risks stated.** The two or three facts that would change the answer.
- **Decision request and next step** with owner and date.
- **Standalone.** No undefined abbreviations, no references to "page 7", no chart that needs explanation.
- **One page.** If it does not fit, the synthesis is not finished ([Synthesis](synthesis.md)).
- **Numbers tie** to the deck and the current model version.

## Step by step

1. Copy the governing thought and key-line statements from the synthesis pyramid.
2. Write the SCQA lead-in in at most four short sentences.
3. Put the answer directly after it, as a sentence a board member could repeat.
4. Under each key-line statement, add the one number that proves it.
5. Add the total effect against the target, and what remains open.
6. Add risks: what would change the answer.
7. Add the decision request: what, budget, owner, start.
8. Test standalone readability: give it to someone outside the team. Can they state the recommendation and the reason in two sentences?
9. Tie every number to the model.

## Common mistakes

- **Table of contents in prose.** "This document covers the market, the customers and the options." Struck: it promises an answer and delivers none.
- **Background first.** Half the page on history before the recommendation appears.
- **Findings without a recommendation.** Five true statements and no "therefore".
- **Hiding the gap.** Presenting moves that reach 80% of the target as if they reach all of it. The board finds out later and stops trusting the rest.
- **Precision beyond the evidence.** "EUR 12.48m" from take-up assumptions with a 25% to 50% range. Use rounded values with ranges ([Weak Evidence](weak-evidence.md)).
- **Numbers that differ from the deck.** One mismatch makes the reader doubt every number.

## Worked example

*Fictional example. Company, figures and people are invented.*

Weak: "Alder Pumps has a long tradition in industrial pumps. Over the past weeks, we analysed the market, interviewed customers and assessed options including Poland, service and pricing. This document presents our findings in four chapters."

Strong:

**Alder should fund a service-contract offer and raise list prices by 4% in 2027; together they add about EUR 19.5m and reach the EUR 200m target, with almost no cushion.** Revenue has stayed at EUR 180m for three years while the board targets EUR 200m by 2030 with at least 9% EBIT margin and no acquisitions.

1. 52% of the 60,000 installed pumps are older than eight years and have no service contract.
2. Customers value a 48-hour response at about EUR 1,000 per pump per year; at 40% take-up this adds about EUR 12.5m a year by 2030 (range EUR 8m to 15.6m) for an investment of EUR 6m.
3. List prices are 6% below the two main competitors; a 4% increase adds about EUR 7m.
4. Poland would add less than EUR 5m by 2030 against two local players with 78% share; we recommend not entering before 2029.

The target is met only in the base case; any shortfall in take-up or price realisation misses it. The answer changes if take-up stays below 25% or a competitor offers the same response time first.

**Decision requested:** approve EUR 6m for the service offer (owner: head of service, start April 2027) and the 4% price increase from July 2027.

## Output template

```markdown
# Executive summary: <question>

**<Governing thought: what the client should do, with the total effect against the target.>**
<Situation and complication in two to three sentences.>

1. <Key-line statement 1, with its number>
2. <Key-line statement 2, with its number>
3. <Key-line statement 3, with its number>

<Remaining gap or open point.> The answer changes if <fact 1> or <fact 2>.

**Decision requested:** approve <what>, budget <amount>, owner <role>, start <date>.

<!-- Checks: one page / standalone / numbers tie to model v<n> -->
```
