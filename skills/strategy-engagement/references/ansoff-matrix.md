# Ansoff Matrix

## What it is

A 2x2 that classifies growth options by product (existing or new) and market (existing or new): market penetration, market development, product development and diversification. Risk and the need for new capabilities rise as an option moves away from today's products and customers. It informs which growth options belong on the long list and how much proof each one needs. It cannot tell you how big an option is, whether it is profitable, or whether the company can execute it.

## When to use

- Building a complete long list of growth options at the start of a [Growth Strategy](growth-strategy.md) case.
- Checking that a growth plan is not over-reliant on one quadrant.
- Explaining to a board why options differ in risk and evidence required.

## When NOT to use

- **Sizing or ranking options.** The matrix has no numbers. Use [Market Sizing](market-sizing.md), [Business Case](business-case.md) and [Sensitivity Analysis](sensitivity-analysis.md).
- **The growth gap is small and clearly in the core.** If a pricing reset closes the gap, a [Pricing Analysis](pricing-analysis.md) is the right tool.
- **Portfolio allocation across existing business units.** Use [Growth-Share Matrix](growth-share-matrix.md) or [Three Horizons](three-horizons.md).
- **Classification disputes.** If the team argues whether a move is "new market" or "existing market", the boundary is unclear. Define products and markets precisely or drop the grid.

## Step by step

1. **Define "existing" precisely.** Input: current revenue by product and customer segment. Output: a written definition of today's products and markets.
2. **State the growth gap.** Input: target and base case. Output: revenue or profit gap by target year.
3. **Generate options per quadrant.** Output: at least one option per quadrant, so none is left out by habit.
4. **Rate distance from the core.** For each option, list which capabilities, channels and customers are new. Output: a distance rating that replaces the crude four-way split.
5. **Size roughly.** Output: order-of-magnitude contribution to the gap per option with its main assumption.
6. **Choose what to analyse.** Output: shortlist with the analysis each option needs before a decision.

## Inputs needed

- Revenue by product and segment; growth target and timing.
- Capability and channel inventory.
- Rough market data for candidate markets.

## Output produced

- A long list of options placed on the grid, with distance from the core and rough size.
- A shortlist and the analyses needed for each.

## Common mistakes

- **Grid as answer.** "Pursue market development" is a category, not a recommendation.
- **Empty quadrants.** Not looking at diversification is fine; not having asked is not.
- **Diversification treated as one thing.** A related move using existing plants and channels differs greatly from an unrelated one. Rate the distance.
- **No link to the gap.** Options listed without asking whether together they close the target gap.

## Worked example

*Fictional example. Company, figures and people are invented.*

Alder Pumps: revenue EUR 180m, board target EUR 200m by 2030, gap EUR 20m.

| Quadrant | Option | Rough 2030 revenue | Main assumption | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Penetration | 4% list-price increase | EUR 7m | Prices 6% below the two main rivals; little volume loss | Analyse |
| Product development | Service contracts on installed base | EUR 12.5m (8 to 15.6m) | 40% take-up at EUR 1,000 per pump per year | Analyse |
| Market development | Entry into Poland | Under EUR 5m | Two local players hold 78% | Killed |
| Diversification | Pumps for hydrogen electrolysis | Unknown | New technology and customers | Watch, no spend |

Service and pricing together give about EUR 19.5m, which reaches the target in the base case with almost no cushion. That thin margin becomes an explicit question for the board.
