# Seven-S Framework

## What it is

A checklist of seven interdependent elements of an organisation: strategy, structure, systems, shared values, style, staff and skills. It tests whether an organisation can carry out a given strategy, and what must change for it to do so. It informs implementation decisions and the risk section of a recommendation. It cannot tell you which strategy is right, and it gives no weights or order between elements.

## When to use

- After a recommendation exists: can this organisation deliver it?
- Operating-model and reorganisation work, to stop the team from changing only the org chart.
- Post-merger integration, to compare two organisations element by element.
- Diagnosing why a sound strategy is not being executed.

## When NOT to use

- **No strategy is defined yet.** Alignment needs a target. Answer the strategic question first.
- **The question is a specific process or cost.** Use value chain or a process map.
- **As a culture survey.** Seven boxes filled from a few workshops describe the organisation without testing anything. Tie every element to what the strategy demands.
- **Short, contained changes.** A pricing change with no new roles or systems rarely needs a full alignment check. A short list of required changes will do.

## Step by step

1. **State the strategy in one sentence.** Input: the recommendation. Output: what the organisation must do differently, with targets.
2. **Describe the current state of each element.** Input: org chart, incentive plans, IT landscape, interviews, headcount data. Output: two or three factual lines per element.
3. **Describe the required state of each element.** Output: what each element must look like for the strategy to work.
4. **Mark the gaps.** Output: for each element, gap size (none, small, large) with the evidence.
5. **Check interdependencies.** Output: gaps that depend on each other, for example a new structure that fails without new incentives.
6. **Turn gaps into actions and risks.** Output: owner, sequence and cost for each large gap; gaps without a fix appear as risks in the recommendation.

## Inputs needed

- The strategy or recommendation being tested.
- Org chart, incentive and bonus schemes, management processes, IT systems.
- Headcount and skills data; interviews across levels, not only the top team.

## Output produced

- A current-versus-required table for the seven elements with gaps rated.
- A short list of interdependent gaps and the actions or risks they create.

## Common mistakes

- **Describing without a target.** A seven-box description of today is not an analysis.
- **Only hard elements.** Structure and systems get fixed while incentives and management style keep the old behaviour.
- **Opinions as evidence.** "Culture is risk-averse" needs examples: decisions taken, projects stopped, how bonuses are paid.
- **All seven equally weighted.** Usually two or three gaps decide whether the strategy works. Say which.
- **No cost or time.** Closing a skills gap takes hiring and months. Leaving that out makes the business case look better than it is.

## Worked example

*Fictional example. Company, figures and people are invented.*

Strategy: Alder Pumps sells service contracts with 48-hour response to its installed base, targeting about EUR 12.5m revenue at 40% take-up.

| Element | Today | Required | Gap |
|---|---|---|---|
| Strategy | Sell new pumps | New pumps plus recurring service | Large |
| Structure | Service is a cost centre under sales | Service unit with its own P&L | Large |
| Systems | No installed-base register; bonus on new-pump revenue | Register of 60,000 pumps; bonus includes contract revenue | Large |
| Shared values | Pride in engineering the product | Pride in keeping customers running | Small |
| Style | Central decisions by the owner family | Regional service leads decide on dispatch | Small |
| Staff | Field engineers sized for reactive repair | Enough engineers for 48-hour response | Large |
| Skills | Repair know-how | Planned maintenance, contract selling | Small |

Interdependency: the bonus scheme and the cost-centre structure together push sales to ignore contracts. Without both changes the take-up target is at risk. These two go into the board paper as preconditions.
