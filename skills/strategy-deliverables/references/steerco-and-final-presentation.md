# Steering Committee and Final Presentation

## Purpose

Get a decision, not applause. The steering committee meeting is where the client's decision body accepts, changes or rejects the recommendation. Most of the work happens before the meeting: stakeholders who see the answer for the first time in the room tend to defend themselves instead of deciding.

## Entry criteria

- The final storyline passed partner review with no open blocker ([Partner Review Checklist](partner-review-checklist.md)).
- The decision to be taken is written as a resolution that can be accepted or rejected.

## Step by step

1. **Write the decision request** as the last page and the first line of the pre-read: "The committee is asked to approve ...". Include budget, owner and start date.
2. **Map stakeholders.** For each member: their stake in the answer, likely objection, and what they need to agree.
3. **Pre-wire.** Walk each key stakeholder through the findings one to one before the meeting. Collect objections, adjust facts that are wrong, and note objections that stay. Nobody important should be surprised in the room.
4. **Send a pre-read** two to three working days ahead: executive summary ([Executive Summary](executive-summary.md)) and the main deck. Not the appendix.
5. **Plan the meeting time.** Roughly a third for the story, two thirds for discussion and decision. Put the decision request on the agenda by name.
6. **Prepare the hard questions.** For each remaining objection, a one-page answer in the backup. Also prepare the honest answer to "What would make this wrong?"
7. **Run the meeting.** Start with the answer, not the method. Show the decision request early. Steer discussion to the decision, park side topics visibly.
8. **Close with a recorded decision:** approved, approved with changes, deferred with a date and the information needed, or rejected with reasons.
9. **Send the decision log** within 24 hours: decision, owners, dates, open items.

## Inputs needed

- Final deck and executive summary.
- Committee members, their roles and history with the topic.
- Time slot and agenda.

## Output produced

- Stakeholder map with pre-wiring notes.
- Pre-read package.
- Decision log and next steps.

## Exit criteria

- Every key stakeholder was pre-wired; remaining objections are known and answered in backup.
- The meeting ended with a recorded decision, or a dated deferral with named missing information.
- The decision log went out within 24 hours.

## Common mistakes

- **Presentation without a decision request.** The meeting ends with "interesting, thank you" and nothing happens.
- **Surprise in the room.** A stakeholder learns that their division looks bad from slide 6, in front of their peers.
- **Method first.** Twenty minutes on approach before the answer appears; the committee loses patience.
- **Defending instead of listening.** Treating every question as an attack instead of information.
- **No written decision.** Two weeks later, members remember different outcomes.

## Worked example

*Fictional example. Company, figures and people are invented.*

Alder Pumps board, March 2027. Decision request: "Approve EUR 6m to build a service-contract offer with 48-hour response, owner: head of service, start: 1 April 2027, and a 4% list price increase effective 1 July 2027." Pre-wiring shows the head of sales fears that the price increase will cost key accounts; the team adds a backup page with the price benchmark per account segment and a phased increase for the top 20 accounts. The founder asks about Poland; the backup shows the market-share data and the condition for revisiting in 2029. Result: approved with one change (phased price increase). The decision log goes out the next morning.
