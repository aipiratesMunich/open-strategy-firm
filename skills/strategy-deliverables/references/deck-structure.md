# Deck Structure

## What it is

The order and size of a recommendation deck: answer first, then the key line in sections, then next steps, then the appendix. The structure mirrors the synthesis pyramid, so a reader who stops after the executive summary has the answer, and a reader who goes deeper finds the proof in the expected place.

## When NOT to use

- **The decision fits on one page and the audience reads.** Send a [One-Page Memo](one-page-memo.md). A deck adds production time and invites skimming.
- **Status updates.** A progress meeting needs a short status page (done, next, issues, decisions needed), not a full storyline.
- **Workshops.** Exercises, canvases and open questions follow the agenda of the session, not an answer-first pyramid.
- **Pure data handover.** A data book or model output is reference material; give it an index and skip the storyline.

## Standard

**Order**
1. Title page with the question, not a topic: "Which growth moves should Alder fund in 2027?"
2. Executive summary: the full answer on one page ([Executive Summary](executive-summary.md)).
3. Decision request, if a decision is wanted in the meeting (can sit on the summary page).
4. One section per key-line statement, in the order of the argument. Each section opens with a divider or a section summary whose title is the key-line statement.
5. Risks and what would change the answer.
6. Next steps: who, what, by when.
7. Appendix: method, detailed data, backup for expected questions.

**Rules**
- Three to five key-line sections. More means the synthesis is not done ([Synthesis](synthesis.md)).
- A tracker on every content page shows the section.
- Titles read alone, in order, tell the full story ([Storyline and Ghost Deck](storyline.md)).
- Every main-deck page supports a key-line statement; everything else goes to the appendix.
- Numbers tie across executive summary, sections and model.

**Length guidance** (main deck, excluding appendix)

| Setting | Main deck | Time split | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Board or steering committee | 8 to 15 pages | One third presenting, two thirds discussion | Sent as pre-read; summary must stand alone |
| Executive working session | 15 to 25 pages | About half presenting | More analysis pages, open questions allowed |
| Team working session | No fixed limit | Mostly discussion | Draft pages, hypotheses marked |
| Pre-read only, no meeting | 5 to 10 pages | n/a | Consider a memo instead |

These ranges are working conventions, not rules from a study. The test is whether every page earns its place.

## Step by step

1. Start from the final synthesis pyramid: governing thought, key line, support.
2. Write the executive summary page first.
3. Create one section per key-line statement, with its statement as section title.
4. Place action-titled pages under each section ([Action Titles](action-titles.md)); order them as the argument needs, usually the strongest evidence first.
5. Move every page that does not support a key-line statement to the appendix.
6. Add risks, next steps and the decision request.
7. Add trackers, page numbers and an appendix index.
8. Count pages against the setting. Cut from the main deck, not from the summary.
9. Run the titles-only read-through and the number tie-out.

## Common mistakes

- **Method before answer.** Ten pages on approach and data before the first finding. The board wants the answer on page two.
- **Chronological sections.** "Phase 1 findings, Phase 2 findings" follow the project plan, not the argument.
- **Appendix in the main deck.** Every analysis the team did gets a page because it was work. Struck because it buries the key line.
- **No next steps.** The deck ends on a finding and nobody owns the follow-up ([Unanswered So-What](unanswered-so-what.md)).
- **Section without a statement.** Divider pages titled "Market" or "Operations" signal topics, not arguments.
- **Same deck for every audience.** A 40-page working deck sent to the board as pre-read.

## Worked example

*Fictional example. Company, figures and people are invented.*

Weak structure for the Alder Pumps board: 1 Title "Growth strategy project", 2 Agenda, 3-6 Approach and interviews, 7-14 Market overview, 15-20 Poland, 21-26 Service, 27-30 Pricing, 31 Summary. The answer appears on page 31; Poland, which was killed, takes six main pages.

Strong structure, 12 pages:

- Page 1: title "Which growth moves should Alder fund in 2027?"
- Page 2: executive summary with decision request.
- Pages 3 to 5, section 1: "The installed base is the largest untapped revenue pool."
- Pages 6 to 7, section 2: "Customers will pay for a guaranteed 48-hour response."
- Pages 8 to 9, section 3: "List prices can rise 4% without losing volume."
- Page 10: "Service and pricing add about EUR 19.5m, enough to reach the EUR 200m target in the base case with almost no cushion; Poland is not worth entering before 2029."
- Page 11: risks and what would change the answer.
- Page 12: next steps.
- Appendix: interview method, price benchmark detail, Poland market-share data, model summary.

## Output template

```markdown
# Deck outline: <client>, <audience>, <date>

**Question on title page:** <question>
**Setting:** board / executive working session / team session. **Target length:** <n> pages

| Page | Section (key-line statement) | Action title | Tracker | Main / appendix |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Title | <question> | | main |
| 2 | Executive summary | <governing thought> | | main |
| 3 | 1. <statement> | | 1 | main |
| | Risks and what would change the answer | | | main |
| | Next steps and decision request | | | main |
| A1 | Appendix | | A | appendix |

**Titles-only read-through passes:** yes / no
**Numbers tie to model version:** <v>
```
