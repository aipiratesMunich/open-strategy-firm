# Action Titles

## What it is

The title of a slide written as the conclusion the slide proves, in one full sentence. The reader gets the message in the few seconds they give each page, and the titles read in order tell the whole story. The client reads them first; the partner reviews them first.

## When NOT to use

- **Appendix and backup tables.** A reference page can carry a descriptive label ("Price list by pump type, Q2 2026"); it proves nothing on its own.
- **Divider, agenda and tracker pages.** They orient; a sentence there adds noise.
- **Early workshop material meant to open a question.** When the client has not seen the data and the team does not yet know the answer, a question title ("Where is the installed base losing value?") is more honest than a premature claim. Switch to action titles once the hypothesis is tested.
- **Ghost deck titles shown to the client as findings.** Write them as action titles internally, but mark them as hypotheses until proven ([Storyline and Ghost Deck](storyline.md)).

## Standard

- **Full sentence** with subject and verb. A noun phrase is a label.
- **Finding plus implication.** States what is true and, where the page is on the key line, what it means for the decision ([So-What Test](so-what-test.md)).
- **Two lines at most** at the deck's title font size. If it needs three, the page makes two points.
- **Numbers where they exist.** Magnitude and comparison in the title: "6% below the two main competitors" beats "below competitors".
- **The body proves exactly the title.** Nothing in the title the chart does not show; nothing in the chart the title ignores.
- **Neutral, plain words.** No adjectives that do the arguing ("dramatic", "significant") when the number can.
- **Consistent with neighbours.** Same terms and units as the titles before and after; read together they form the storyline ([Pyramid Principle](pyramid-principle.md)).

## Step by step

1. Write the finding as a plain sentence with its number and comparison.
2. Ask "so what for the decision?" once. If the answer is short, add it to the title after a comma or colon. If it needs its own argument, it belongs on the next page.
3. Cut every word that does not change the meaning: "It can be seen that", "Analysis shows".
4. Check length at real font size. Over two lines: shorten or split the page.
5. Check the body against the title. Adjust whichever is wrong.
6. Read all titles of the section in order. Fix gaps and repetitions.

## Common mistakes

- **Topic label.** "Market overview" tells the reader to do the thinking. Struck because the page has no claim to test.
- **Title the chart cannot prove.** "Customers will pay for uptime" above a chart of downtime costs. Cost of downtime is not willingness to pay; struck as a logic gap (logic gaps).
- **Two messages joined by "and".** "Prices are low and Poland is crowded." Split into two pages.
- **Hedged into nothing.** "Service may potentially offer some opportunity." If the evidence is weak, say so with a range; do not blur the sentence.
- **Number-free claim where a number exists.** "Prices are below competitors" when the benchmark gives 6%. The number is the message.
- **Method as title.** "We interviewed 12 plant managers" reports the project, not the finding.

## Worked example

*Fictional example. Company, figures and people are invented.*

Alder Pumps, weak versus strong titles:

| Weak | Strong |
|---|---|
| Installed base analysis | 52% of the 60,000 installed pumps are older than eight years and have no service contract |
| Service potential | At 40% take-up, service contracts add about EUR 12.5m revenue a year by 2030 (range EUR 8m to 15.6m) |
| Pricing benchmark | List prices are 6% below the two main competitors; a 4% increase is worth about EUR 7m a year |
| Poland | Two local players hold 78% of the Polish market, so entry would add less than EUR 5m by 2030 |
| Customer interviews | Two of three lost customers left over repair response time, which a 48-hour guarantee addresses |

Read top to bottom, the strong column already tells the board what to fund and what to drop.

```markdown
| # | Section | Action title (full sentence, max two lines) | Number and comparison in title | Body proves title (yes/no) | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | <key line> | <finding>, <implication> | <value> vs. <comparison> | | hypothesis / proven |
| 2 | | | | | |

**Titles-only read-through tells the story:** yes / no, <gap>
```
