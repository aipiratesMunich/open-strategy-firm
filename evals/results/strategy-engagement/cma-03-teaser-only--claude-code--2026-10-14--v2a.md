## Turn 1

**User:** A banker sent Halvorsen a teaser for "Project Heron", a German precision-sensor maker. Asking 8x EBITDA. Teaser says adjusted EBITDA 2025 EUR 5.0m (reported EUR 3.8m, EUR 1.2m of "one-off" items added back), revenue EUR 31m, "strong pipeline". Should we bite? Board call is Thursday.

One flag up front: **you cannot set a price off this teaser — and that itself is the board answer for Thursday.** The playbook rule here is explicit: when all you have is seller/banker figures with no data-room access, you don't produce a valuation or a maximum price; you decide what to request and what would make it a no. Anchoring any number to the 8x figure now would mean valuing the target to justify the seller's ask rather than the buyer's own view — exactly the trap to avoid.

# M&A screening: Halvorsen, should we pursue "Project Heron" at the teaser's 8x EBITDA ask?

**Answer:** Do not proceed to a price or exclusivity. Authorize, at most, a **non-binding indication of interest conditional on data-room access** — no number attached, or a wide range explicitly marked indicative. Set the internal walk-away logic only after the add-backs are itemized and verified and a haircut-plan standalone valuation exists.
**Confidence:** High on "don't commit to a price yet" (the teaser structurally cannot support one). Low on everything else — strategic fit, synergies, deal-breakers are all unknown.
**Specialists and review:** Financial due diligence on the EBITDA bridge; legal on change-of-control/key-person clauses typical of German Mittelstand sensor makers; someone outside the deal team to sanity-check before the board gets attached to this target.

## Why this teaser can't be priced yet

| Component | What the teaser gives | What's missing |
|---|---|---|
| Reported EBITDA 2025 | EUR 3.8m (12.3% margin on EUR 31m revenue) | 3–5 year trend, not just one year |
| Adjustment | +EUR 1.2m "one-offs" → EUR 5.0m adjusted (16.1% margin) | Line-by-line schedule, each item's nature and recurrence history |
| Asking price | "8x EBITDA" | **8x which EBITDA?** 8x adjusted = ~EUR 40.0m EV. 8x reported = ~EUR 30.4m EV. That's a **EUR 9.6m swing** resting entirely on how the seller chose to characterize EUR 1.2m of costs |
| "Strong pipeline" | One adjective | No backlog, no committed orders, no win-rate data — pure narrative |

The add-back is 32% of reported EBITDA. That's large enough to matter and vague enough to distrust by default — "one-off" items in a sale process routinely include recurring costs relabeled as exceptional (owner comp normalization can be legitimate; restructuring, bad-debt, or related-party rent reclassifications often aren't). Until you see the schedule, treat EUR 5.0m as the seller's claim, not a fact — so the de facto asking multiple could be anywhere from 6.1x to 8x on real earnings, before you've even started negotiating.
