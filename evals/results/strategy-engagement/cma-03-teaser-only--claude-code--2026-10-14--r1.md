## Turn 1

**User:** A banker sent Halvorsen a teaser for "Project Heron", a German precision-sensor maker. Asking 8x EBITDA. Teaser says adjusted EBITDA 2025 EUR 5.0m (reported EUR 3.8m, EUR 1.2m of "one-off" items added back), revenue EUR 31m, "strong pipeline". Should we bite? Board call is Thursday.

Short answer: don't decide "buy or pass" off this teaser — decide "proceed to next stage or not." A one-page teaser at this stage of the process isn't enough to value the business; it's enough to decide whether it's worth the NDA and a proper look.

A few things in this specific teaser I'd flag to the board before Thursday:

**The multiple is being quoted on the wrong base.** 8x is applied to adjusted EBITDA (€5.0m). On reported EBITDA (€3.8m) the same asking price is actually ~10.5x. The adjustments are 31% of reported EBITDA — that's a large addback ratio for a single year, and "one-off" items that size are often partly recurring (owner comp normalization, recurring restructuring, bad debt, etc.). Ask for a full bridge of what's in the €1.2m, line by line, before anchoring on 8x at all.

**Only one year is shown.** No 2023/2024 actuals, no margin trend. A single flattering year dressed up as "2025E/A" is a classic teaser pattern. Ask for 3–5 years of P&L and the same EBITDA-adjustment methodology applied consistently across all of them — you want to see if adjustments are a one-time thing or a habit.

**"Strong pipeline" is a sales line, not data.** Ask for order book/backlog figures and customer concentration (top 5 customers as % of revenue) — German precision-sensor suppliers are frequently auto/industrial-concentrated, which is a real risk right now.

**Missing entirely: net debt, capex.** Precision-sensor manufacturing is capex-intensive; EBITDA without a capex/maintenance-capex view overstates cash generation. You need net debt (EV vs. equity value) and historical capex before 8x means anything.

**Why is it for sale now?** Sell-side teaser, sell-side banker — no indication of seller motivation (succession, distress, strategic exit). Worth asking directly.

For Thursday: I'd frame the ask to the board as "authorize signing the NDA and requesting the CIM/management presentation with a QoE-style breakdown of the adjustments and 3-year history" — not "approve 8x" or "pass." That keeps optionality open at near-zero cost and is the normal next step in a sell-side process anyway.
