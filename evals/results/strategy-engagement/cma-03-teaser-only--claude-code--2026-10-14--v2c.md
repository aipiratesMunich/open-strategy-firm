## Step 3: Deciding analyses

**a. Screening funnel** — N/A; this is a single banker-brought target, not a field screen. Flag the trap: don't let "the banker brought us this" substitute for asking whether Heron is even the best use of acquisition capacity in this space.

**b. Target market and position** — Cannot assess from the teaser. "Strong pipeline" is banker language with zero evidence behind it — it is not a market-growth figure, a backlog number, or a win-rate. Data request: order backlog, bookings trend last 3 years, customer-by-customer revenue, win/loss rate. **c. Strategic fit map** — Cannot complete without Halvorsen's stated goal (open question above). Provisionally: if the rationale is sensor-technology-for-instruments, this looks like related diversification (Ansoff: new product, adjacent capability) rather than market extension — worth testing once the goal is confirmed.

So-what: the deal's attractiveness swings 24% on EUR 1.2m of claimed add-backs that nobody outside the seller has seen itemized. That is the single number that decides whether this is a fair-priced asset or a 10.5x deal dressed as an 8x deal. "One-off" items in a seller-prepared teaser routinely include things that recur every year (owner's above-market comp normalized down, a "one-off" bad debt or inventory write-down that shows up again next year, deferred maintenance capex reclassified). Until there's a line-by-line schedule with cash evidence, treat adjusted EBITDA as **unverified, seller-asserted** — grade it weak evidence.

**e. Synergy case** — Cannot be built without Halvorsen's integration thesis (open question). Flag now: any revenue synergies the banker or Heron management pitch in a CIM go in as upside only, never into the price.

**f. Deal-breaker check** — Not yet testable, but name what to ask for immediately, because German Mittelstand precision-sensor sellers very often carry exactly these:
- Customer concentration (is one OEM >20-30% of revenue, and is there a change-of-control clause?)
- Key-person dependence (founder/owner often *is* the engineering or sales relationship — ask for org chart and tenure of top 5 technical/commercial staff)
- Works council / German labor-law constraints on any post-close restructuring
- Dual-use export control: precision sensors (depending on spec — e.g., inertial, optical, high-precision measurement) can fall under German/EU dual-use export regulation (BAFA); this affects both customer base (export licenses) and deal structuring. Flag for counsel early, not at the end of diligence.

**g. Outside view** — No comparable-deal data in hand. Do not accept the seller's banker's own comps unchallenged.
