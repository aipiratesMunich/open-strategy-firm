# com-02: Outsourcing what customers buy you for, and one span for all

## Prompt

> Halvorsen's board has decided: outsource field calibration and move everyone to a 1:12 span. Please design the new organisation and the implementation plan.

Input file: `com-02-input.md`

*Fictional company. All figures invented for evaluation.*

## What this case tests

The trap: two board measures that the skill's trap checks reject. Customers choose Halvorsen for field calibration (62% of wins), and the pilot shows outsourcing raised churn by 9 points, which costs more contribution than the saving. A uniform span ignores work type: R&D at 1:5 is specialist work, service centre at 1:15 is already wide. The skill must not implement the mandate as given; it must show the numbers and propose the smallest change that meets the cost goal.

## Reference values

- Extra churn from the pilot: 14% - 5% = 9 points (36 of 400 pilot customers).
- Applied to about 3,000 field customers: 3,000 x 9% x EUR 18,000 = about EUR 4.86m revenue a year at risk.
- Contribution at risk: 4.86 x 45% = about EUR 2.19m a year, above the EUR 1.8m saving (net about -EUR 0.39m a year, before churn compounds).
- Span 1:12 everywhere would cut field teams (13) and the service centre (15) to narrower spans, adding managers, and widen R&D (5) and sales (6), whatever the work needs.

## Pass criteria

- Must: states early that the outsourcing measure conflicts with the evidence: customers choose Halvorsen for on-site calibration (62% of wins).
- Must: quantifies the pilot churn effect (9 points) and the contribution at risk (about EUR 2.2m a year) against the EUR 1.8m saving, with the arithmetic shown.
- Must: flags the pilot as one region and one year (evidence strength medium at best) and proposes how to confirm it, without inventing other data.
- Must: rejects a single 1:12 span and shows that spans should differ by work type, using the four units in the input (noting 1:12 would narrow field and service spans and widen R&D and sales).
- Must: proposes the smallest change that serves the board's cost goal (e.g. keep field calibration in-house, look for savings in standardised activities, partner only for low-value or remote calibration with churn monitoring), with options compared against criteria agreed or flagged for agreement.
- Must: counts transition cost and management attention and gives a sequence.
- Must: flags labour law and works council consultation for specialists.
- Must: answer line states what changes, in what sequence, by when, and says on the first line that the mandate as written is not recommended.
- Must not: produce an implementation plan for outsourcing field calibration and a uniform 1:12 span as requested.
- Must not: invent peer spans or outsourcing benchmarks.
