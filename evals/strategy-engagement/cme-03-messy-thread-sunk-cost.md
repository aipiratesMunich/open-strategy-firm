# cme-03: Messy email thread, conflicting numbers, sunk cost pressure

## Prompt

> Forwarding you the Poland thread from Corvina. Can you work the case for Marc? Don't have more than this right now.

Input file: `cme-03-input.md`

*Fictional company. All figures invented for evaluation.*

## What this case tests

Robustness. The input is a forwarded thread: conflicting market sizes with different scopes, a missing attachment, sunk costs used as the reason to go, and a CEO who wants "how" only. The skill should reopen "whether" without losing the client, reconcile or flag the numbers, keep sunk costs out of the decision, and still produce a useful answer page with open questions.

## Reference values

- Budget EUR 1.5m includes the country manager at EUR 110k a year (about EUR 0.26m for the 28 months from September 2026 to end 2028, if counted from the budget).
- Bar: EUR 3m revenue by end 2028. Against the study's EUR 48m (three cities), that is about 6% of the segment within about two years.
- If the German 31% contribution margin applied, EUR 3m revenue would give about EUR 0.93m contribution a year; this is an untested transfer.

## Pass criteria

- Must: reopens "whether" alongside "how" (e.g. "before choosing a mode, check the EUR 3m bar can be met") in a way that respects the CEO's request.
- Must: states that the EUR 60k study and the hire already made are sunk and do not justify entry; the country manager's future cost does count against the budget.
- Must: flags the conflict between EUR 48m (study, three cities, office wholesale) and "80m+" (unsourced, unknown scope), uses the study as the documented base, and asks for the missing study attachment.
- Must: puts the EUR 3m bar in proportion (about 6% of the three-city segment by 2028) and says this needs a bottom-up ramp from distributor capacity or sales reps, not a share assumption.
- Must: compares distributors and own sales on cost, speed, control and exit cost, using only the input and flagged assumptions.
- Must: flags the German 31% margin as not tested for Poland.
- Must: answer is enter under named conditions, enter later, or do not enter, with exit criteria; not an unconditional "go".
- Must: open-questions table naming who can answer (e.g. CFO for the study, Tomasz for distributor terms).
- Must not: invent Polish market data, competitor names ("the Italians" stays unnamed) or distributor margins as facts.
- Must not: treat "we have already hired and paid" as a reason in the recommendation.
