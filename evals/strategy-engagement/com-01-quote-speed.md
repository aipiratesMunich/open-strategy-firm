# com-01: Slow quotes, a new division proposed, decision rights at fault

## Prompt

> Kestrel wants faster quotes to win key accounts. The sales director proposes a new Key Account division. Work the operating model case from the diagnostic pack; answer page for the CEO.

Input file: `com-01-input.md`

*Fictional company. All figures invented for evaluation.*

## What this case tests

The happy path: a strategy with a measurable performance level, a process walk, a decision map and a proposed structure. The evidence points to approvals that run on every quote although the written rules limit them to large or non-standard quotes. The skill should start from what the strategy requires, fix process and decision rights before structure, state decision rights explicitly and prefer the smallest change.

## Reference values

- Elapsed 6.0 days, hands-on work 1.0 day: about 83% of elapsed time is waiting.
- Approvals and the pricing queue (2.0 + 1.5 + 1.0 + 0.5 = 5.0 days elapsed) carry 0.4 days of work.
- For standard quotes (70%), applying the written rules removes MD, finance and legal steps: intake 0.5 + pricing 0.5 = 1.0 day if the queue is removed or prioritised, meeting the one-day target.
- Win rate within 2 days 34% vs 5 days or more 17%: speed is linked to wins (association, not proof of cause).
- Proposed division: EUR 3.2m a year and about 40 FTE.

## Pass criteria

- Must: opens from the strategy requirement (standard quotes within one working day, win rate 22% to 30%) before any structure discussion.
- Must: shows that waiting, not work, dominates (about 5 of 6 days) and that most waiting sits in approvals and the pricing queue.
- Must: identifies that approvals happen on every quote although written rules limit them to quotes above EUR 250k or non-standard terms.
- Must: recommends explicit decision rights for the key decisions (e.g. pricing team releases standard quotes within a rate card; MD only above EUR 250k; finance only beyond 30 days; legal only for non-standard terms; a named owner of the customer after signing).
- Must: shows that the process and decision-rights fix reaches about one day for standard quotes without the new division, and keeps the division as one option with its cost (EUR 3.2m) and transition risk.
- Must: two or three options scored against criteria agreed before scoring (or proposed criteria flagged for agreement).
- Must: treats the win-rate link as an association to monitor, not proof.
- Must: names the soft elements to change (e.g. MD incentives or habits, pricing team capacity) and a sequence with dates.
- Must: answer line states what changes, in what sequence, by when.
- Must not: recommend the Key Account division as the first move.
- Must not: invent peer spans, peer quote times or industry win rates.
