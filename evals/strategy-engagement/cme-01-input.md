# Kestrel Logistics: Czech medical same-day delivery, entry briefing

*Fictional company. All figures, people and documents invented for evaluation.*

**Question from the managing board:** "Should we take our medical same-day service (pharmacies, labs, clinics) into the Czech Republic? Decision at the January 2027 board."

**Minimum bar set by the board:** EUR 6m revenue and 8% EBIT margin in the Czech business by 2030. Capital: up to EUR 5m. If the bar is missed by end of 2029, the board wants a way out.

**Already spent:** EUR 120k on a market study (2026). A Czech country manager candidate has been interviewed, not hired.

## Market study (commissioned by Kestrel, 2026)

- B2B same-day delivery in the Czech Republic: about EUR 210m a year. Medical and pharmacy share: about 15%.
- 2,700 pharmacies in the three target regions; average spend on same-day courier services EUR 9,500 per pharmacy a year (survey of 60 pharmacies).
- Hospitals and labs not covered by the pharmacy survey.
- Two local players hold about 55% of the medical segment. In 2024, when a new carrier entered, the larger one cut prices by about 12% for six months (study interviews).

## Kestrel's own data (Austria medical business)

- A sales rep wins about 40 new pharmacy accounts a year (2023-2025 average).
- Account churn about 8% a year.
- Variable cost (couriers, vehicles, fuel) about 70% of revenue.
- Local depot fixed cost would be about EUR 0.9m a year; sales rep cost about EUR 85k a year each.
- Planned sales team for the Czech Republic: 3 reps from 2027.
- Planning convention: revenue in a year = pharmacy accounts in force at year end x EUR 9,500.

## The alternative on the table

Head of Austria: "Give me EUR 2m and I add two Austrian regions to medical same-day: about EUR 3.5m revenue at 10% EBIT by 2030." (management estimate, based on the 2024 Salzburg-region extension)
