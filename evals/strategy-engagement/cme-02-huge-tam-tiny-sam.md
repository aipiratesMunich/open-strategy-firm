# cme-02: Huge headline market, tiny serviceable segment

## Prompt

> Our adviser says the US is a USD 9bn market and we only need 1%. The board likes it. How should Halvorsen enter the US? Notes attached.

Input file: `cme-02-input.md`

*Fictional company. All figures invented for evaluation.*

## What this case tests

The trap: the client asks "how", the headline is "1% of a big market", and the serviceable segment is a small fraction of the headline. The skill must reopen "whether", size what Halvorsen can actually serve, show that 1% of the headline exceeds the entire serviceable segment, and build share bottom-up.

## Reference values

- Benchtop electrochemistry: 9,000 x 2.5% = USD 225m.
- Labs that calibrate in-house: 225 x 20% = USD 45m.
- Reachable through the one distributor: 45 x 30% = USD 13.5m serviceable segment.
- "1% of USD 9bn" = USD 90m, about 6.7 times the whole serviceable segment.
- Even at Halvorsen's best-ever 30% share, revenue would be about USD 4.05m, below the USD 10m minimum, and the German result took twelve years.
- To reach USD 10m inside the distributor's footprint, Halvorsen would need about 74% of the serviceable segment.

## Pass criteria

- Must: reopens "whether" before "how" and states the minimum bar (USD 10m by 2030, USD 4m) as the test.
- Must: narrows the market step by step to about USD 13.5m serviceable (or shows each step with its source and the same logic).
- Must: states that USD 90m exceeds the serviceable segment several times over (about 6.7x) and rejects "1% of a big market" as a method.
- Must: builds a share or revenue estimate bottom-up from the distributor's reach and Halvorsen's own best share, and shows it misses USD 10m.
- Must: answer is do not enter, or enter later under named conditions (e.g. a US calibration partner that unlocks the regulated 80%, a wider distribution footprint), with what would make the board revisit it.
- Must: flags the USD 9bn as an unsourced adviser figure and the 20% and 30% as internal or distributor estimates to verify, without replacing them with invented US figures.
- Must: notes that US certification and regulatory questions go to specialists.
- Must not: propose an entry plan whose base case relies on a percentage of the USD 9bn.
- Must not: cite US market reports, regulators' rules or competitor shares as facts.
