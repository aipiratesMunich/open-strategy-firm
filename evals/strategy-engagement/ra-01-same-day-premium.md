# ra-01: Test a willingness-to-pay hypothesis from pilot data and interviews

## Prompt

> Test this hypothesis for the Kestrel rollout decision: online retailers will pay a premium for same-day delivery. Everything we have is in the evidence pack. Work without web search; tell me what you would still need.

Input file: `ra-01-input.md`

*Fictional company. All figures invented for evaluation.*

## What this case tests

The happy path: a vague hypothesis, a usable kill criterion, revealed behaviour (pilot contracts), stated intent from a biased interview sample, and an unsourced management forecast. A good analyst sharpens the hypothesis, ranks revealed behaviour above stated intent, triangulates the decisive number, records counter-evidence, applies the outside view to the 120-retailer forecast and gives a verdict with its strength.

## Reference values

- Signed at EUR 4.50 or more: 4 of 14 offered = 28.6% (kill threshold 25%).
- Signed at any price: 6 of 14 = 42.9%. Average signed price of all six: EUR 4.55; of the four at EUR 4.50 or more: EUR 4.80.
- Stated intent in interviews: 6 of 8 = 75%, but five of the eight are existing customers; among non-customers 1 of 3.
- Revealed (28.6%) and stated (75%) differ by a factor of about 2.6.

## Pass criteria

- Must: restates the hypothesis as a testable statement with price, unit, share, scope (mid-size online retailers, pilot city) and period.
- Must: computes the revealed share at EUR 4.50 or more as 4 of 14 (about 29%) and compares it with the 25% kill criterion.
- Must: treats the 75% stated intent as weaker than the pilot contracts and names the sample bias (five of eight interviewees are already customers).
- Must: treats the management estimate (120 retailers, EUR 4.80) as a hypothesis, not as a second source.
- Must: has a findings table where every claim carries source, date, unit and scope from the evidence pack.
- Must: lists counter-evidence, including at least two of: competitor at EUR 3.60 (R13), next-day at EUR 2.10 (R07), 3% checkout uptake seen by R07, two of six signings only below EUR 4.50 after discount.
- Must: flags that 14 offers is a small sample and gives a range rather than a point estimate (e.g. "roughly 15% to 45%" or an equivalent stated range).
- Must: for the 120-retailer forecast, states that no comparable rollout is available in the pack and names the reference class to check (e.g. same-day launches by other carriers, take-up in comparable cities), without inventing one.
- Must: verdict is confirmed with weak or medium strength, reshaped (e.g. a narrower segment or lower price point) or open; the verdict line states the strength.
- Must: ends with what would settle it: a data request or interview guide (e.g. lost-deal interviews with non-customers, second-city test).
- Must not: report 75% as the share of retailers willing to pay.
- Must not: rate the evidence strong.
- Must not: cite external studies, market reports, URLs or benchmark figures that are not in the input.
