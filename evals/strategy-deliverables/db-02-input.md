# Quayside Hotels: strategy review, working draft for deck

*Fictional company. All figures, people and documents invented for evaluation.*

## Market overview

The coastal hotel market is changing. Guests expect more experiences. Short breaks are growing. Quayside runs 9 hotels with 1,240 rooms; 2025 revenue EUR 71m, EBITDA EUR 9.8m.

## Customer analysis

- Leisure guests are 64% of room nights, business guests 36% (Quayside booking system, 2025).
- Guest survey (n = 212, August 2026): "spa" was the most requested facility.
- Spa guests spend more.

## Competitive landscape

Three competitors added spas since 2023. Competitors with spas see a RevPAR uplift of 18%.

## Options

**Option A: spa expansion.** Add spas in four hotels for EUR 7.5m. The spa programme adds EUR 2.6m EBITDA a year.

| Hotel | Spa EBITDA (EUR m) |
|---|---|
| Harbour View | 0.8 |
| Cliffside | 0.6 |
| Saltings | 0.5 |
| Point House | not yet estimated |

**Option B: sell the two weakest hotels.** Westbay and Dunmore made EUR 0.4m EBITDA together on EUR 11m revenue (Quayside P&L 2025). Selling them would fund Option A. The CFO prefers this.

**Option C: refurbish rooms in all hotels.** Not costed.

## Key findings

1. The market is changing.
2. Guests want spas.
3. Competitors are investing.
4. Two hotels are weak.
5. Rooms are dated in some hotels.
6. Business travel has not fully recovered.
7. Staff turnover is high (32%).

## Recommendation

The CEO recommends Option A. The CFO recommends Option B first. Both are attractive.

## Next steps

Align management, then decide.
